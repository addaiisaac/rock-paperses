01 · Conviction

From busyness to intentionality.

The cycle is digital, and it is relentless. A notification arrives dressed as urgency. A feed offers rest and charges attention for it. Deadlines multiply until prayer is the thing that can wait, and tomorrow is always the day faith will become serious. Young professionals and students are not short on belief. They are short on an unguarded hour.

Busyness

  • Deadlines that never finish
  • Feeds that pretend to rest you
  • A tomorrow that keeps moving

becomes

Intentionality

  • One hour you actually keep
  • Prayer returned to the day
  • Work held, not worshipped

Hustle culture rarely argues with God. It simply never leaves a chair empty for Him.

  • The excuse

    “I will be serious about God after this season.”

  • The cost

    Attention given away in pieces too small to notice, until the soul feels far away.

  • The invitation

    One kept hour is enough to begin again.

    Boundaries, not shame

    Digital Discipline

    Screens are not the enemy. Unexamined attention is. Choose one boundary for seven days. Keep it small enough to actually keep.

    The First Hour

    Before the inbox and before the group chat, give God the first quiet of the day. Leave the phone in another room until you have prayed or read, even for ten minutes. The morning is often the only hour a student or a young professional truly owns. Do not hand it to a lock screen.

    The Dock

    Choose one physical place for your phone that is not your hand, your bed, or your Bible. A shelf. A bag by the door. During meals, Scripture, and conversation, the device lives there. You cannot love well what you refuse to look at. Attention is a beginning of love.

    The Sabbath Scroll

    Choose one evening a week when the feeds stay closed. Not as punishment, and not as a performance. As a reminder that you are more than what you can refresh. Walk, call someone, or sit with a psalm. The world will still be posting when you return.
